### Step 2 — Predict interviewer follow-up questions

For EVERY important resume point, generate questions in increasing difficulty.

Use this structure:

#### Level 1 — Basic understanding

Questions such as:

* What is X?
* Why did you use X?
* How does X work?
* What problem does X solve?
* What are its advantages/disadvantages?

#### Level 2 — Implementation

Questions such as:

* How did you implement it?
* How was it integrated into your application?
* What components were involved?
* What happened internally?
* How did data flow through the system?

#### Level 3 — Why this approach?

Ask:

* Why did you choose X instead of Y?
* What alternatives did you consider?
* What trade-offs did you make?
* What would happen if X failed?
* What limitations does your approach have?

#### Level 4 — Deep technical follow-ups

Ask questions that an experienced interviewer could use to determine whether I actually worked on the project.

Examples:

* What happens internally?
* Where can race conditions occur?
* What happens under high load?
* What happens if a dependency becomes unavailable?
* How do retries interact with timeouts?
* How do you prevent duplicate processing?
* What happens during partial failure?
* How do you handle concurrency?
* What happens when multiple instances process the same request?
* How would you debug this issue in production?

#### Level 5 — Scenario/design questions

Create realistic scenarios based on my resume.

For example:

"If your service receives 5,000 requests/sec and the downstream vendor starts returning errors, what happens?"

"Two requests try to modify the same resource simultaneously. How do you prevent inconsistent data?"

"Your Kafka consumer crashes after processing a message but before acknowledging it. What happens?"

"Redis becomes unavailable. How does your application behave?"

Make the scenarios specific to the technologies and claims in MY resume.

### Step 5 — "Did you actually build this?" questions

Act like a skeptical interviewer.

For every major technology in my resume, ask questions that distinguish someone who:

A. merely knows the technology,

B. used the technology,

C. actually implemented/debugged/maintained it in production.

For example:

Instead of only asking:

"What is a circuit breaker?"

also ask:

"Where exactly was the circuit breaker placed in your request flow?"

"What configuration did you use and why?"

"What happens when the circuit opens?"

"How does HALF_OPEN work?"

"What happens if retries are also enabled?"

"Could retries make the problem worse?"

"How did you monitor it?"

### Step 6 — Cross-question me

Interviewers often ask follow-up questions based on my previous answer.

Therefore, create question chains.

Example:

Q1: Why did you use Redis?

If I answer Redis improves latency:

Q2: What was the cache key?

Q3: What was the TTL?

Q4: How did you handle stale data?

Q5: What happens when Redis goes down?

Q6: What happens if two instances update the same key?

Q7: Did you use distributed locking?

Q8: Why Redis instead of database caching?

Create similar chains for my actual resume topics.
